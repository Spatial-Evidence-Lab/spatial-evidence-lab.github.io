Who is SEL and what does it investigate?

About

Independent geospatial research.

Spatial Evidence Lab is an independent geospatial research initiative using GIS, spatial analysis, data engineering and cartography to investigate how people, services, environments and infrastructure vary across places.

01 · What is SEL?

Research about place

Spatial Evidence Lab develops spatial evidence about people, places and spatial systems, treating geography as a central part of the research question.

IDENTITY

Independent research

SEL is an independent research initiative that develops place-based evidence through geographic data, spatial analysis and cartographic interpretation. The work is organised as a research portfolio rather than as a consultancy or service catalogue.

APPROACH

Spatial evidence

Research connects geographic information with analytical reasoning: defining the study area, selecting meaningful measures, comparing spatial conditions and interpreting patterns in their geographic context. Maps are used to communicate results, not as substitutes for the underlying evidence.

OUTPUT

Research that can be inspected

Each project is organised so that its question, geography, indicators, methods and outputs can be followed as a chain of evidence. Where data access and licensing permit, supporting datasets, scripts, maps and documentation are made available for inspection and reuse.

03 · Geographic scope

Research across place

SEL works across local places, Ireland and international case studies, adapting geographic scale and level of detail to the question being investigated.

LOCAL PLACES

Detailed spatial questions

Small-area and local studies examine variation within towns, neighbourhoods and communities, allowing research to connect broad indicators with the conditions experienced at a finer geographic scale.

IRELAND

National spatial evidence

National datasets support comparison across counties, regions, settlements and small areas, making it possible to identify geographic differences and broader spatial patterns within Ireland.

INTERNATIONAL CASE STUDIES

Comparative research

Selected international studies apply the same evidence-led approach in different geographic and planning contexts, allowing methods to be tested against contrasting environmental, demographic and spatial conditions.

04 · Research principles

Evidence with context

SEL approaches evidence through clear questions, explicit geography, transparent assumptions and documented analytical choices, with openness and reproducibility where practical.

01

Evidence-led

Research questions and findings are grounded in identifiable geographic and statistical evidence, with measures chosen to represent the conditions being investigated.

02

Spatially explicit

Location, geographic context, scale and spatial relationships are treated as analytical information rather than simply as map presentation choices.

03

Transparent

Data sources, analytical choices, assumptions and limitations are documented so that results can be interpreted in relation to how the evidence was produced.

04

Reproducible

Where practical, workflows, scripts, GIS projects and supporting materials are structured so that another researcher can follow the analytical chain and reproduce key outputs.

05

Open

Research outputs, derived data and analytical resources are shared where source conditions and licensing permit, helping make the evidence base more accessible for further work.

05 · Research in the open

Open, documented research

Projects, methods, data and supporting materials are connected so that the relationship between evidence, analysis and published outputs can be understood.
