Spatial data

Data

The evidence behind the analysis.

Our research brings together geographic, environmental, demographic, socioeconomic and infrastructure data to understand how places differ, change and interact.

01 Data across place

Context matters
across space.

Spatial evidence is only meaningful when its geographic and temporal context is understood.

We therefore consider where data comes from, what area it covers, the period it represents, its spatial resolution and the limitations that may affect interpretation.

Our datasets may include

Different questions require different forms of geographic evidence. Dataset selection is guided by the research question, geography, analytical scale and available evidence.

  • 01Population and demographic data
  • 02Quality-of-life indicators
  • 03Transport and accessibility data
  • 04Land-use and land-cover data
  • 05Environmental and climate data
  • 06Natural-hazard data
  • 07Housing and socioeconomic data
  • 08Renewable-energy and infrastructure data
02 Data sources

Know the source
behind the data.

Research projects draw on data from a range of authoritative and specialist sources.

For each dataset, where information is available, we aim to document its provenance, geographic and temporal context, resolution, format, licence and project use.

03 Data & research quality

Evidence requires
critical context.

Data quality is an important part of spatial analysis.

We consider differences in geographic boundaries, resolution, temporal coverage, definitions and measurement when combining datasets.

Where limitations or uncertainties may affect interpretation, they are documented as part of the research process.

A map is only as reliable as the evidence, assumptions and geographic context behind it.
