What evidence does SEL use?

Data

The evidence behind the research.

Spatial Evidence Lab works with geographic, demographic, socioeconomic, environmental, transport, infrastructure and other spatial datasets. We document where evidence comes from, what it represents, how it is structured, and how it is used in research.

01 Data landscape

Evidence across place
and time.

Different research questions require different forms of geographic evidence. SEL brings together datasets that describe people, places, environments and spatial systems.

Data selection is considered in relation to the geographic question, spatial unit, temporal reference and evidence available for the study.

01

Population & demography

Population structure, distribution and demographic characteristics.

02

Housing & socioeconomic conditions

Housing, income, employment and related social and economic evidence.

03

Transport & accessibility

Transport networks, public transport, access and mobility evidence.

04

Land use & land cover

Land-use patterns, settlement, land cover and geographic context.

05

Environment & climate

Environmental conditions, climate-related evidence and natural systems.

06

Natural hazards

Spatial evidence relating to exposure, hazards and environmental risk.

07

Energy & infrastructure

Energy potential, infrastructure and the systems that support places.

08

Geographic boundaries

Administrative, statistical and other spatial units used to locate evidence.

02 Data sources

Know the source
behind the evidence.

The evidence base draws on authoritative public bodies, open-data infrastructures, specialist datasets and other documented sources.

The catalogue identifies the source, geographic and temporal context, format, licence and research use where that information is available.

01 · NATIONAL STATISTICS

Central Statistics Office

Census and official statistical evidence, including small-area population and socioeconomic data.

03 Data documentation

Know what the data
actually represents.

Dataset documentation provides the context needed to understand and reuse spatial evidence appropriately.

Documentation is maintained at dataset or project level according to the evidence available and the requirements of the research.

01

Geographic coverage

Countries, regions, localities or other geographic areas represented by the dataset.

02

Temporal coverage

The reference date, period or update cycle represented by the evidence.

03

Spatial resolution

The geographic level, unit or spatial detail at which information is available.

04

Format & structure

How data is provided, represented and prepared for use in spatial research.

05

Project use

The research projects or evidence products in which a dataset is applied.

04 Provenance & metadata

Trace the evidence
from source to use.

Provenance records help distinguish source evidence from processed, derived and published research products.

Where available, SEL documents source identity, version, ownership, geographic reference, lineage and other metadata needed to understand reuse.

05 Open data & licensing

Reuse where
conditions permit.

Open research depends on being clear about the conditions under which data and derived materials can be accessed, reused and redistributed.

Licensing and access conditions are therefore treated as part of the dataset context rather than assumed to be identical across sources.

DATA ACCESS

Open where permitted

Source availability and access conditions vary by provider and dataset.

LICENSING

Check the source terms

Reuse, attribution and redistribution requirements should be checked against the original source.

RESEARCH OUTPUTS

Share supporting evidence

Where conditions permit, project datasets, documentation and outputs are made available through the research repository.
