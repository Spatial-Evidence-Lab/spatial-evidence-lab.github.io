SEL-002 · Spatial Accessibility Analysis

Bandon Public Transport Accessibility

Where does public transport accessibility fall short — and where might that matter most? This analysis combines walking access, scheduled service and population vulnerability to identify potential transport disadvantage across Bandon, County Cork.

Main Result

Where accessibility and vulnerability overlap

The strongest equity signal occurs where lower public transport accessibility overlaps with higher population vulnerability.

Accessibility–Vulnerability Hotspots

Map 01.06 combines the accessibility deficit with population vulnerability to identify locations where poor access may have greater social significance.

What it shows

Priority areas are not simply places with poor access. They are locations where accessibility deficits and vulnerability occur together.

Why it matters

The result shifts the question from “where is access poor?” to “where might poor access matter most?”

Use

The hotspots provide a screening layer for further investigation of pedestrian connections, stop provision and public transport service.

Interpret with care

Hotspots indicate potential transport disadvantage. They are not investment decisions and should be tested against local knowledge, site conditions, stakeholder evidence and operational feasibility.

01 · Research

Three questions

The analysis moves from physical access to effective service accessibility and finally to transport equity.

RQ01

Where can people realistically walk to public transport?

Walking-network analysis measures practical access to stops within 5, 10 and 15 minutes.

RQ02

Where is effective accessibility weaker?

Walking accessibility is combined with scheduled peak-time service to move beyond stop proximity alone.

RQ03

Where may transport disadvantage matter most?

Accessibility deficits are assessed alongside population vulnerability to identify potential equity hotspots.

05 · Method

A compact, reproducible workflow

The analysis separates network access, service provision and population vulnerability before integrating them into an equity-focused screening measure.

Input 01

Census 2022

Population characteristics provide the demographic evidence used to represent spatial differences in potential transport vulnerability.

Input 02

Public Transport / GTFS

Stop, route and scheduled service information defines the transport network and service-frequency dimension.

Input 03

Walking Network

Network analysis represents practical walking access to public transport rather than relying solely on straight-line distance.

01 Define study area and transport network
02 Generate 5 / 10 / 15-minute walking access
03 Derive walking accessibility and service frequency
04 Combine accessibility dimensions
05 Screen accessibility–vulnerability hotspots
Hotspot screening model
Hotspot Score = 0.40 × Access Deficit + 0.60 × VULnorm
Higher values indicate greater combined screening priority.
Interpretation & limitations

The outputs are a spatial screening framework rather than a final investment appraisal. Results should be considered alongside current service conditions, local knowledge, detailed site assessment, stakeholder evidence and operational feasibility.

07 · Planning

What could happen next?

The analysis is intended to identify where more detailed investigation may be useful — not to prescribe individual interventions.

01

Investigate pedestrian connections

Use hotspot areas as a screening layer for footpath continuity, crossings, lighting and safer walking routes to public transport.

02

Review stop and network coverage

Examine areas with weaker walking access for potential stop, feeder or routing improvements where demand and operations support intervention.

03

Examine service frequency

Assess whether scheduled peak-time service corresponds with areas identified as having weaker effective accessibility.

04

Repeat the analysis over time

The workflow can be rerun as Census, GTFS and walking-network data are updated, creating a basis for ongoing accessibility monitoring.

Conclusion

From access to equity

Public transport accessibility in Bandon is shaped by more than distance to a bus stop. Walking connectivity, scheduled service and population characteristics interact to create different levels of potential accessibility and transport disadvantage.

The analysis indicates a broad central-to-peripheral pattern, with stronger composite accessibility generally associated with the central urban area and weaker accessibility extending toward peripheral residential locations.

The final equity screen adds the social dimension: places with lower accessibility are not necessarily the places where poor access may matter most. Combining accessibility deficits with population vulnerability provides a more targeted basis for further investigation.

Next research

Potential extensions include detailed temporal service analysis, destination accessibility, population-demand modelling, observed travel-behaviour validation and scenario testing for proposed stops, routes or service changes.
