Bandon Public Transport Accessibility Analysis

Public transport accessibility, population vulnerability and equity-led spatial prioritisation

Bandon, County Cork, Ireland, is a commuter town approximately 30 km southwest of Cork City and a gateway to West Cork.

This spatial evidence study examines how walking access, scheduled service frequency and population vulnerability interact across Bandon.

KEY FINDING — Public transport accessibility is spatially uneven.Bandon's town centre and central corridor benefit from stronger walking access and scheduled service frequency, while several peripheral residential areas experience lower accessibility. Priority increases where accessibility deficits overlap with greater population vulnerability.

01 — Research Questions

Framing accessibility as a spatial, temporal and equity-related problem.

RESEARCH QUESTIONS

Is public transport a viable alternative to private-car commuting — and where is potential transport disadvantage concentrated?

The study assesses public transport accessibility through walking-network access and scheduled service frequency, then examines how these patterns intersect with Census 2022 population vulnerability.

01 · Commuter viabilityAssess whether public transport provides a viable commuting alternative between Bandon and Cork City.
02 · Potential vulnerabilityIdentify areas where limited accessibility coincides with characteristics associated with greater potential transport dependence.

05 — What the Evidence Shows

Cross-map synthesis of the seven analytical outputs.

01 · CENTRAL ACCESS

Accessibility is strongest around Bandon town centre and principal public transport stops.

02 · CORE–PERIPHERY

Walking accessibility generally declines toward peripheral residential areas.

03 · FREQUENCY MATTERS

Proximity to a stop does not necessarily mean equivalent effective accessibility.

04 · VULNERABILITY

Population vulnerability is spatially differentiated and does not necessarily coincide with the best-served areas.

05 · EQUITY PRIORITY

The highest-priority locations emerge where access deficits coincide with greater vulnerability.

06 — Planning Implications

Using the maps as evidence for further investigation and prioritisation.

Walking & pedestrian infrastructure

Investigate footpath connectivity, crossings, lighting and access corridors linking peripheral residential areas with central stops.

Bus stop network

Assess candidate locations for additional stops or local feeder / loop connections in areas outside the strongest walking catchments.

Peak service frequency

Use the frequency-adjusted results to support assessment of morning and evening peak service provision and commuter competitiveness.

GIS monitoring

Repeat the workflow using updated GTFS and Census information to monitor accessibility and potential equity priorities.

Interpretation note: Hotspots are candidate priorities for further investigation. They should not be treated as definitive investment decisions without local validation, stakeholder knowledge and operational feasibility assessment.

08 — Conclusion & Next Research

From Bandon evidence to a wider Irish spatial research programme.

CONCLUSION

Accessibility should be understood as a spatial and multidimensional condition.

The Bandon analysis demonstrates how walking-network accessibility, scheduled service frequency and population vulnerability can be integrated into a coherent small-area evidence framework. The most meaningful planning questions emerge where accessibility conditions intersect with population vulnerability.

01 · REAL-TIME ACCESSIBILITY

Incorporate operational and real-time service reliability.

02 · TIME OF DAY

Extend beyond peak periods to evenings and weekends.

03 · ESSENTIAL DESTINATIONS

Assess access to schools, healthcare and other opportunities.

04 · ACTIVE TRAVEL

Integrate walking and cycling infrastructure quality.

05 · IRELAND QoLI

Connect transport accessibility with the wider Small Area Quality of Life Index.

06 · REPLICATION

Test the workflow in other Irish towns and settlement contexts.
