Bandon Public Transport Accessibility & Vulnerability Analysis

A reproducible GIS-based spatial evidence study on transport equity and regional connectivity in Bandon, Co. Cork.

Download Full PDF Report View Map Series

Executive Summary & Core Questions

Bandon functions as both a commuter town for Cork City and a gateway to West Cork[cite: 2]. This study evaluates whether public transport offers a viable alternative to car ownership and identifies local areas subject to transport disadvantage[cite: 2].

RQ1: Is public transport a viable alternative to private cars?

Partially yes. Public transport is highly viable along the central urban corridor with high service frequencies[cite: 2]. Viability drops significantly in peripheral residential areas where walking distances are longer and scheduled coverage is sparse[cite: 2].

RQ2: Which areas are most vulnerable to transport deficits?

Outer residential peripheries. Critical hotspots occur where lower accessibility intersects with higher concentrations of older adults, car-less households, disabled residents, and long-term unemployed populations[cite: 2].

Refined Analytical Methodology

The analysis follows a 6-stage reproducible GIS pipeline processing spatial data, schedules, and Census demographics[cite: 2]:

  1. Pedestrian Network Catchments: Generates 5, 10, and 15-minute network service areas around GTFS bus stops using OpenStreetMap topology[cite: 2].
  2. Temporal Service Frequency: Extracts peak morning trips from NTA GTFS feeds to calculate schedule-adjusted service intensity[cite: 2].
  3. Composite Accessibility Index (CAI):** Integrates physical network walking scores and service frequency into a 5-tier classification[cite: 2].
  4. Vulnerability Index Normalisation: Standardises Census 2022 Small Area indicators into a unified population need score[cite: 2].
  5. Equity Hotspot Modeling: Overlays transit access deficits against population vulnerability using weighted scoring[cite: 2].
  6. Policy Prioritisation Matrix: Categorises Small Areas into actionable priority levels (Critical, High, Moderate, Low, Well Served)[cite: 2].

Population Vulnerability Framework

Population need is evaluated using 15 CSO Census 2022 Small Area variables structured into four distinct analytical domains[cite: 2]:

Vulnerability Domain Census 2022 Variables Included Transport Equity Relevance
Demographic T1_1AGE70_74T, T1_1AGE75_79T, T1_1AGE80_84T, T1_1AGEGE_85T, T4_5RP/T8_1_RT Captures elderly residents and retirees with reduced physical mobility or lower driving rates[cite: 2].
Health & Mobility T8_1_UTWSDT, T12_1_T, T12_3_BT, T12_3_VBT Identifies populations requiring step-free, accessible transport and shorter walking radii[cite: 2].
Socio-Economic T8_1_LTUT Highlights areas of long-term unemployment where affordable transit provides essential access to jobs[cite: 2].
Transport Reliance T15_1_NC, T11_1_BUT, T11_2_T1-T8 Measures households without private cars, current bus commuters, and morning peak departures[cite: 2].

* Total population (T1_1AGETT) serves as the standardising denominator[cite: 2].

Model Formulation & Scoring

To prioritize social equity, the primary model assigns a 60% weighting to population vulnerability and a 40% weighting to physical transport access deficits[cite: 2]:

1. Access Deficit Calculation:

$$\text{Access Deficit} = 1 - \text{Normalised CAI}$$

2. Primary Model (Equity-Led Prioritisation):

$$\text{Hotspot Score}_{\text{Equity}} = 0.4 \times \text{Access Deficit} + 0.6 \times \text{VUL}_{\text{norm}}$$

3. Sensitivity Model (Balanced Split):

$$\text{Hotspot Score}_{\text{Balanced}} = 0.5 \times \text{Access Deficit} + 0.5 \times \text{VUL}_{\text{norm}}$$
