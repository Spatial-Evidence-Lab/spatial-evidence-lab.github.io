Key finding

Accessibility is strongest where network reach, service frequency and lower vulnerability overlap.

The analysis points to a central-to-peripheral pattern: some locations have good physical access to public transport, while others combine weaker access with characteristics that may increase dependence on transport services.

Interpretation Public transport appears capable of supporting some Bandon–Cork journeys, but the evidence does not establish a universal private-car replacement because door-to-door journey time, capacity, fares and observed commuting behaviour are outside this analysis.

01

Research questions

The analysis moves from physical network access toward an equity-focused understanding of where transport disadvantage may matter most.

RQ01

Is public transport a viable alternative to private-car commuting between Bandon and Cork City?

The analysis tests whether the combination of walking access and scheduled peak-time service provides a credible spatial basis for public transport use, while recognising that journey time, fares, capacity and observed travel behaviour require further assessment.

RQ02

Which areas are potentially most vulnerable to limited public transport accessibility?

The analysis identifies locations where accessibility deficits overlap with demographic, health, employment or household characteristics associated with greater potential transport dependence.

04

What the evidence shows

The results answer two linked questions: whether public transport can function as a practical alternative for some Bandon–Cork trips, and where limited accessibility may carry greater social significance.

01

ACCESS → VIABILITY

Public transport is a credible option for part of the Bandon–Cork commuter market

Where homes are within practical walking reach of stops and those stops are supported by scheduled peak-time service, public transport has the spatial conditions needed to serve commuter trips. The analysis therefore supports a viable alternative for some locations, rather than a universal replacement for private-car travel.

02

ACCESS → CORE–PERIPHERY

Effective accessibility becomes weaker away from the best-connected urban areas

Walking-network access and scheduled service do not remain spatially uniform across Bandon. Peripheral residential areas are more likely to experience longer walking connections, weaker network reach or lower service accessibility.

03

SERVICE → OPPORTUNITY

Stop proximity alone overstates the practical opportunity to use public transport

A nearby stop provides physical access, but the availability of scheduled peak service determines whether that access translates into a usable transport opportunity. Accessibility is therefore a combination of reach and service.

04

VULNERABILITY → DEPENDENCE

Potential exposure to limited access is socially differentiated

Demographic, health, employment and household characteristics vary across the study area. Locations with greater potential transport dependence therefore warrant attention even when their accessibility deficit is not the most extreme.

05

EQUITY → PRIORITY

The strongest priority signal occurs where access deficits and vulnerability overlap

The final equity screen shifts the analysis from simply mapping poor access to identifying places where limited public transport may have greater consequences for residents. These are priority areas for validation, not automatic investment decisions.

NETWORK→WALKING→SERVICE→VULNERABILITY→EQUITY

05

Spatial typology

Three broad spatial conditions help interpret the combined accessibility pattern across Bandon.

TYPE 01

Well-Served Core

Central urban areas where walking access and public transport service are comparatively strong.

  • Shorter walking distances
  • Greater network connectivity
  • Stronger composite accessibility
TYPE 02

Transitional Residential Areas

Areas where accessibility remains available but becomes more dependent on walking connections and service characteristics.

  • Intermediate walking access
  • Variable service availability
  • Potential localised deficits
TYPE 03

Peripheral Estates & Margins

Outer residential locations where access, service and vulnerability may combine to create greater potential disadvantage.

  • Longer walking access
  • Lower composite accessibility
  • Potential hotspot conditions

HOTSPOT SCREENING MODEL

Hotspot Score = 0.60 × VULnorm + 0.40 × Access Deficit

Higher values indicate greater combined screening priority across Bandon's Small Areas, combining population vulnerability with public transport access deficits.

Interpretation principle

Accessibility is multidimensional

Proximity to a public transport stop is not treated as a complete measure of accessibility. Walking connectivity and scheduled service both contribute to effective access.

Equity principle

Need is considered alongside access

The hotspot stage introduces population vulnerability so that lower-access areas can be distinguished according to their potential social significance.

Limitation

Screening evidence rather than a final investment decision

Results should be interpreted alongside local knowledge, current service conditions, detailed site assessment, operational feasibility and stakeholder engagement.

08

What could this evidence support?

The analysis provides a screening framework for identifying where further transport and public-realm investigation may provide the greatest potential benefit.

01

Pedestrian infrastructure

Investigate footpath connectivity, lighting and safer crossings along access corridors to central public transport stops.

02

Bus-stop network & micro-routing

Evaluate additional stops, feeder arrangements or local loop routing where weaker walking access and demand justify intervention.

03

Peak service frequency

Examine whether peak-period service levels adequately support movement between Bandon and Cork City and whether deficits relate to service availability.

04

Ongoing accessibility monitoring

Institutionalise the workflow and rerun it as updated GTFS, Census and network data become available.

Planning principle

Use the hotspot map as a starting point for investigation — not as a substitute for local knowledge.

From access to equity

Public transport accessibility cannot be understood from stop proximity alone.

The Bandon analysis shows how the interaction between the transport network, walking connectivity, scheduled service frequency and population characteristics creates different spatial conditions of access.

The final equity analysis adds a planning dimension: areas with poor accessibility are not necessarily the same as areas where poor accessibility may have the greatest social significance. Combining accessibility deficits with vulnerability provides a more targeted basis for screening potential transport disadvantage.

Next research

Future development could extend the analysis through more detailed temporal service analysis, population-demand modelling, accessibility by destination type, validation against observed travel behaviour and scenario testing for proposed stop, routing or service interventions.
