01 · Research overview

Accessibility as a spatial evidence problem.

Public transport planning in regional commuter towns frequently relies on radial distance buffers that can overlook pedestrian barriers, micro-topographic constraints, and limitations in scheduled service frequency. This study examines the spatial conditions under which residents of Bandon, Co. Cork, can access and make effective use of the regional public transport network. The geographic scope comprises 34 Census Small Areas (CSAs), with a total aggregated population of 9,863 residents, of whom 8,196 reside within the Bandon Urban Area (BUA). By separating physical network accessibility and GTFS-derived scheduled service availability from demographic need, the analysis first identifies objective transport deficits independently of population characteristics. Demographic vulnerability is then incorporated at the final spatial-screening stage to identify areas where transport deficits coincide with greater levels of population need.

Question 1

How does the spatial accessibility of public transport for travel between Bandon and Cork City vary across the town?

The analysis models network-based walking times to principal stops alongside GTFS scheduled peak-period service frequencies. It maps intra-town spatial disparities in transit supply rather than determining absolute car competitiveness.

Question 2

Which areas are potentially most vulnerable to limited public transport accessibility because of their demographic, health, employment, or household characteristics?

The framework identifies geographic zones where severe accessibility deficits directly coincide with elevated concentrations of age, health, disability, economic status, and low household car availability proxies derived from Census 2022.

Core Concept Accessibility ≠ Proximity

Physical proximity to a bus stop does not guarantee effective transit access if scheduled service is infrequent or non-existent during peak operational windows. Conversely, a longer pedestrian journey to a primary corridor stop can yield significantly higher effective accessibility due to superior service frequency and regional route connectivity.

TRANSPORT SYSTEM

Supply

Evaluates physical network accessibility to principal stops and scheduled GTFS peak-period frequency.

POPULATION CONTEXT

02 · Study area

Bandon, County Cork.

Bandon is a commuter town situated approximately 30 km southwest of Cork City along the N71 corridor. The urban morphology is defined by a historical commercial core along the River Bandon, flanked by expanding peripheral residential areas characterized by undulating terrain, narrow network pinch points, and variable footpath continuity.

The principal study population is 8,196 residents within the defined Bandon BUA. The underlying indicator dataset contains 34 CSO Small Areas with a combined Census population of 9,863.

These figures are deliberately distinguished. Some Small Areas intersect the BUA only partially and extend beyond the study boundary. The 9,863 figure therefore represents the complete source Small Areas, while 8,196 represents the population located within the BUA study boundary.

This geographic distinction is important when interpreting the indicators: Small Area measures describe the characteristics of their source statistical units and are not estimates of only the population falling inside the BUA boundary.

04 · Methodology

Separate the dimensions. Then examine the overlap.

The model deliberately separates transport-system performance from population need. Pedestrian Accessibility and Service Availability form the Composite Accessibility Index (CAI); Population Vulnerability is calculated independently and introduced only when constructing the final hotspot score.

01

Census indicators

Extract selected Census 2022 indicators across the study area.

02

Normalised Population Vulnerability

Construct Elderly, Health, No-Car and Economic indicators, and normalise using min–max normalisation.

03

Pedestrian Accessibility

Calculate network walking bands using ArcGIS Network Analyst along OpenStreetMap topology at 5 km/h.

04

Service availability

Extract scheduled peak-period departure frequencies and headways from GTFS feeds.

05

Composite Accessibility Index (CAI)

Combine Pedestrian Accessibility and Service Availability using 50:50 weighting.

06

Access Deficit

Convert normalized accessibility to relative access deficit: using AD = 1 − CAI_norm.

07

Hotspot screening

Combine Access Deficit and Normalized Population Vulnerability using 40:60 weighting.

Population Vulnerability Index (VUL)

30 / 30 / 20 / 20

30% Elderly Population · 30% Health & Disability · 20% No-Car Households · 20% Economic Vulnerability.

Pedestrian Accessibility

0–5 / 5–10 / 10–15 / >15 min

< 417 m · Score = 1.0 · >417–833 m · Score = 0.6 · >833–1,250 m · Score = 0.3 · >1,250 m · Score = 0.

Composite Accessibility Index (CAI)

50 / 50

50% Pedestrian Accessibility + 50% scheduled Service Availability. Population Vulnerability is not included in the CAI.

Final Hotspot Score (HS)

40 / 60

40% Access Deficit + 60% VUL_norm. The result is a spatial screening measure, not a direct investment decision.

Methodological guardrail.

Selected Census categories may overlap at the individual level (e.g., an elderly resident who also reports a disability). Composite values represent model-derived spatial concentrations rather than unique counts of vulnerable individuals, direct measures of personal transport dependence, or observed unmet travel demand. The resulting Hotspot Score is a spatial screening tool to direct secondary investigation.

11 · Limitations & future research

Interpret the evidence within its limits.

The findings are relative spatial measures produced from defined datasets, geographies, modelling assumptions and weighting choices. They are designed for screening and evidence development rather than as direct measurements of individual behaviour or investment need.

01

Walking assumptions

A 5 km/h walking speed is used. Individual walking ability, gradients, weather, safety and route preference are not modelled.

02

Network representation

Mapped stops, routes and pedestrian connections depend on available datasets and require local verification where site conditions matter.

03

GTFS schedules

Scheduled timetable information does not represent real-time delay, cancellation or observed passenger waiting time.

04

Census geography

Census 2022 Small Areas were redrawn; comparison with earlier releases requires explicit geographic harmonisation.

05

Population Vulnerability

The index is a model-derived spatial proxy, not an individual-level measure of transport dependence or observed demand.

06

Relative normalisation

Scores are relative within the Bandon analytical population and should not be interpreted as absolute measures.

07

Demand dynamics

The framework measures potential spatial access to scheduled supply, not realised individual trips, destination accessibility or private-car competitiveness.

08

Composite weighting

The 50:50 CAI weighting and 40:60 hotspot weighting are analytical assumptions; different weights can produce different spatial results.

Future research: real-time and temporal expansion

Integrate real-time data and evaluate off-peak, evening and weekend accessibility.

Future research: essential destinations

Extend the accessibility framework to schools, healthcare and other essential destinations.

Future research: active travel

Integrate walking and cycling infrastructure quality, safety and connectivity.

Future research: Quality of Life Index

Develop transport accessibility as a spatial quality-of-life dimension within the Ireland Small Area Quality of Life Index.

Future research: regional replication

Replicate the workflow across comparable Irish regional towns such as Clonakilty, Skibbereen, Bantry, Midleton and Mallow.

12 · Conclusion

A structured spatial framework for public transport accessibility.

The Bandon analysis demonstrates how network walking accessibility, scheduled service availability and Census-based population vulnerability can be brought together in a transparent and reproducible GIS workflow.

FINDING

Accessibility is spatially differentiated.

The central area generally has stronger transport-system accessibility, while several peripheral residential areas experience longer network walking journeys and/or weaker scheduled service availability.

RESULT

Four areas show strong overlap.

Four Small Areas meet both the Access Deficit > 0.75 and VUL_norm > 0.75 screening thresholds, with Hotspot Scores between 0.856 and 0.947.

CONTRIBUTION

Separate evidence before integration.

The framework keeps transport-system accessibility and population vulnerability analytically distinct before examining their spatial overlap, supporting transparent evidence-led screening.

Research contribution Reproducible GIS-based spatial evidence

The workflow provides a structured basis for identifying locations that warrant further pedestrian, service or flexible-transport investigation. Any intervention requires additional engineering, operational, demand, accessibility, cost and feasibility assessment.
