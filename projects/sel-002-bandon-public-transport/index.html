Principal Finding

Public transport accessibility is spatially differentiated across Bandon. The central corridor generally has stronger network walking access and scheduled service availability, while several peripheral residential areas experience longer walking journeys and/or weaker scheduled service availability.

Scope Note: The analysis identifies spatial conditions for further investigation. It does not measure individual transport dependence, observed unmet demand or the feasibility of a specific intervention.
02 · EVIDENCE

Four dimensions of evidence

01 · ACCESS

Walking accessibility

Network travel time from residential areas to principal public transport stops.

Measured through: 5 / 10 / 15 minute network walking bands.
02 · SERVICE

Scheduled service availability

Peak-period service frequency and headway derived from GTFS timetable data.

Measured through: scheduled departures and headway.
03 · VULNERABILITY

Population vulnerability

Relative spatial concentration of selected Census 2022 characteristics.

Includes: older age, health/disability, household car availability and economic indicators.
04 · OVERLAP

Accessibility–vulnerability screening

Access Deficit and Population Vulnerability are combined only at the final stage.

Output: Hotspot Score.

A Key Distinction: Accessibility is not the same as proximity

A location can be close to a bus stop while still having limited effective access if scheduled services are infrequent. The Bandon framework therefore separates network walking access from service availability before combining the two.

Research questions

RQ01

How does accessibility vary across Bandon?

Accessibility is spatially differentiated, with generally stronger walking access and scheduled service availability around the central corridor and weaker conditions in several peripheral residential areas.

RQ02

Which areas potentially experience greater exposure to limited accessibility?

The final screening identifies locations where relatively weaker accessibility overlaps with higher relative population vulnerability.

What the analysis does not establish: The study does not measure individual transport dependence, realised trip behaviour, private-car competitiveness or observed unmet demand.
05 · METHOD

How the model works

CENSUS (30 / 30 / 20 / 20) → POPULATION VULNERABILITY
↓
NETWORK WALKING + GTFS SERVICE → CAI (50 / 50) → ACCESS DEFICIT
↓
ACCESS DEFICIT + VULNERABILITY → HOTSPOT (40 / 60)

Vulnerability

  • 30% Elderly
  • 30% Health & Disability
  • 20% No-Car
  • 20% Economic

Walking

  • 0–5 min → 1.0
  • 5–10 min → 0.6
  • 10–15 min → 0.3
  • >15 min → 0

CAI

  • 50% Pedestrian Access
  • 50% Service Availability

Hotspot

  • 40% Access Deficit
  • 60% Vulnerability

Interpretation Boundary

The indicators describe relative Small Area conditions rather than individual-level characteristics. The Population Vulnerability Index is a model-derived spatial proxy and is not a direct measure of transport dependence or unmet demand. The Hotspot Score is a screening measure for further investigation, not an automatic investment decision.

Reproducibility

The analysis combines Census 2022 Small Areas, GTFS timetable data, OpenStreetMap network data and Tailte Éireann / CSO geography using ArcGIS Pro, Network Analyst and PostGIS.

DATA: Census 2022 · GTFS · OSM · GEOGRAPHY: BUA · Small Areas · ANALYSIS: ArcGIS Pro · Network Analyst

Evidence boundaries

Walking assumptions

5 km/h; no individual ability, weather or safety modelling.

Network data

Mapped pedestrian connections and stops require local verification.

GTFS

Scheduled service does not represent real-time reliability.

Census geography

Small Areas do not perfectly coincide with the BUA.

Relative scores

Normalised values are relative to this Bandon analytical population.

Model weights

Different weighting assumptions could produce different spatial results.
