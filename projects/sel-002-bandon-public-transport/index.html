01 · OVERVIEW

Accessibility as a spatial evidence problem.

Public transport accessibility is not determined by proximity to a stop alone. This study separates network walking accessibility and scheduled service availability from population vulnerability, then examines their spatial overlap at Small Area level.

Walking access, scheduled service, and population vulnerability are modelled as distinct evidence streams. Their spatial relationship is examined only at the final screening stage, keeping the analytical chain transparent.

PRINCIPAL FINDING

Peripheral parts of Bandon contain the clearest spatial combination of weaker transport-system accessibility and higher relative population vulnerability. The final screening identifies four areas for further investigation.

02 · STUDY AREA

Bandon, County Cork.

Bandon is a commuter town approximately 30 km southwest of Cork City along the N71 corridor. The study uses the Bandon Built-Up Area as its principal population geography and 34 CSO Small Areas as the underlying analytical units.

The principal study population is 8,196 residents within the Bandon Built-Up Area (BUA). The underlying indicator dataset contains 34 CSO Small Areas with a combined Census population of 9,863.

These figures represent different geographic extents. Some Small Areas intersect the BUA only partially and extend beyond the study boundary. Small Area indicators therefore describe their complete statistical units, not only the population falling inside the BUA.

The study distinguishes between two principal geographic units:

Bandon Built-Up Area (BUA): Defines the spatial boundary and headline study population, containing 8,196 residents (Census 2022).

CSO Small Areas: Comprises 34 Central Statistics Office (CSO) Small Areas with an aggregate Census population of 9,863 residents, serving as the underlying statistical geography for indicator construction.

Boundary Intersection & Contextual Interpretation

The two population figures represent different spatial extents and must not be directly compared as competing estimates of Bandon’s urban population. Because Small Area boundaries do not align perfectly with the BUA boundary, several Small Areas intersect the study area only partially and extend beyond the BUA perimeter. Consequently, Small Area metrics reflect complete statistical units and function as contextual, area-level proxies rather than exact populations inside the BUA.

03 · EVIDENCE

Four evidence streams. One final overlap.

Each evidence stream has a distinct analytical role. Transport-system accessibility and population vulnerability remain separate until the final screening stage.

01 · ACCESS

Walking accessibility

Network travel time from residential areas to principal public transport stops rather than simple straight-line distance.

Measured through: 5 / 10 / 15 minute network walking bands.
02 · SERVICE

Scheduled service availability

Peak-period scheduled frequency and headway derived from GTFS timetable data.

Measured through: scheduled departures and headway.
03 · VULNERABILITY

Population vulnerability

Relative spatial concentration of selected Census 2022 characteristics.

Includes: older age, health/disability, no-car households and economic indicators.
04 · OVERLAP

Accessibility–vulnerability screening

Access Deficit and Population Vulnerability are combined only at the final stage.

Output: Hotspot Score for further investigation.

04 · METHODOLOGY

Separate the evidence. Then examine the overlap.

Transport-system performance and population need are modelled independently. Population vulnerability enters only when constructing the final screening measure.

VULNERABILITY30 / 30 / 20 / 20

30% elderly population; 30% health & disability; 20% no-car households; 20% economic vulnerability.

WALKING ACCESS0–5 / 5–10 / 10–15 / >15 min

0–5 min → 1.0; >5–10 min → 0.6; >10–15 min → 0.3; >15 min → 0.0. Modelled at 5 km/h using the pedestrian network.

COMPOSITE ACCESSIBILITY50 / 50

50% Pedestrian Accessibility + 50% scheduled Service Availability. Population Vulnerability is not included in the CAI.

FINAL SCREENING40 / 60

40% Access Deficit + 60% VULnorm. The Hotspot Score is a spatial screening measure for further investigation.

Methodological guardrail. Selected Census categories may overlap at the individual level. Composite values represent model-derived spatial concentrations rather than unique counts of vulnerable individuals, direct measures of personal transport dependence or observed unmet travel demand. The 50:50 CAI and 40:60 hotspot weights are analytical assumptions; different weights could produce different spatial results.

RESEARCH QUESTIONS

What the evidence supports.

RQ01

How does public transport accessibility for travel between Bandon and Cork City vary across the town?

Accessibility is spatially differentiated. The central corridor generally has shorter network walking journeys and stronger scheduled peak-period service availability, while several peripheral residential areas have longer walking journeys and/or weaker scheduled service availability.

RQ02

Which areas are potentially most vulnerable to limited public transport accessibility?

The final screening identifies areas where relatively weaker accessibility overlaps with higher relative Population Vulnerability. These are spatial screening locations, not direct measurements of individual transport dependence or observed unmet demand.

Methods & limitations
Walking assumptions

5 km/h; individual ability, gradients, weather, safety and route preference are not modelled.

Network representation

Mapped pedestrian connections and stops require local verification where site conditions matter.

GTFS schedules

Scheduled timetable data does not represent real-time delay, cancellation or observed waiting time.

Census geography

Small Areas do not perfectly coincide with the BUA boundary.

Relative scores

Normalised values are relative within the Bandon analytical population.

Population vulnerability

The index is a spatial proxy, not an individual-level measure of transport dependence.

Demand dynamics

The framework measures potential access to scheduled supply, not realised trips or private-car competitiveness.

Composite weighting

The 50:50 CAI and 40:60 hotspot weights are analytical assumptions.
