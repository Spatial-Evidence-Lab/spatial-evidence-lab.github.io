Research methods

Methods

From spatial data to evidence.

Spatial Evidence Lab uses geographic data, spatial analysis and reproducible workflows to investigate patterns, relationships and change across place.

01 How we work

A structured workflow
for spatial evidence.

Our work begins with a geographic question: where is something happening, how does it vary across place, and what factors help explain the pattern?

We combine spatial datasets, geographic analysis and visualisation to turn complex information into clear, evidence-based findings.

01

Define

Identify the research question, geographic context, analytical objectives and relevant spatial scale.

02

Assemble

Source, assess and integrate geographic, environmental, demographic, socioeconomic and infrastructure data.

03

Prepare

Clean, standardise and transform datasets so that different sources can be analysed consistently.

04

Analyse

Apply appropriate spatial, statistical and geographic techniques to identify patterns, relationships, accessibility, change and spatial variation.

05

Validate

Review results, test assumptions and document uncertainties, limitations and important sources of variation.

06

Communicate

Translate analysis into maps, indicators, comparisons, reports and other forms of spatial evidence.

02 Methods & analysis

Methods selected
for the question.

Methods are selected according to the research question, geographic context, available data and analytical objective.

01

Spatial analysis

Geographic techniques used to understand how conditions vary across space and how places relate to one another.

  • Spatial distribution and pattern analysis
  • Proximity and distance analysis
  • Accessibility analysis
  • Spatial joins and overlays
  • Geographic comparison
  • Network-based analysis

02

Environmental & land-use analysis

Spatial approaches to environmental change, land systems and natural hazards.

  • Land-use and land-cover change
  • Forest loss and deforestation
  • Environmental quality
  • Heat and climate risk
  • Wildfire exposure
  • Coastal inundation

03

Population & socioeconomic analysis

Analysis of the spatial distribution of people, living conditions and socioeconomic characteristics.

  • Population and demographic analysis
  • Quality-of-life indicators
  • Housing conditions
  • Population vulnerability
  • Socioeconomic characteristics
  • Spatial inequalities

04

Energy & infrastructure analysis

Geographic approaches to infrastructure, renewable-energy potential and spatial suitability.

  • Solar-energy potential
  • Wind-energy potential
  • Infrastructure planning
  • Housing and energy relationships
  • Spatial suitability

05

Indicators, scoring & measurement

Where research requires comparative measures, datasets may be transformed and combined using documented rules appropriate to the research question.

  • Indicator construction
  • Normalisation
  • Weighting
  • Scoring
  • Composite indices
  • Comparative measures
03 Reproducible research

Transparent
by design.

Wherever practical, our research is supported by documented data sources, transparent analytical steps and reproducible workflows.

Project methods, assumptions and limitations are documented alongside research outputs so that results can be understood, evaluated and, where data and licensing permit, reproduced.

01

Data sources

Where datasets come from and how they are used in the analysis.

02

Methods

How analytical transformations, calculations and spatial operations are performed.

03

Assumptions

Important decisions, thresholds, weights, uncertainties and limitations.

04

Outputs

Maps, indicators, reports and supporting research materials produced by the work.

Where data and licensing permit, supporting datasets, code, methods and outputs may be made available alongside individual projects.
