How does SEL do research?

Methods

From question to evidence.

Spatial Evidence Lab uses structured spatial research workflows to turn geographic questions into transparent, interpretable evidence. Methods are selected according to the research question, geography, data characteristics and analytical objective.

01 · Research workflow

From question to evidence

SEL begins with a defined geographic question and an explicit analytical objective. The workflow establishes the geography, constructs the evidence, applies an appropriate method, tests the result and communicates the findings.

01

Question

Define the research question, evidence requirements and analytical objectives.

02

Geography

Establish the relevant geographic units, boundaries, spatial context and geographic inputs.

03

Construct

Assemble relevant datasets, boundaries and geographic inputs, documenting provenance and temporal reference.

04

Method

Construct indicators, transformations and spatial models suited to the research question.

05

Test

Analyse spatial relationships, distributions, patterns, accessibility, exposure or change and assess the result.

06

Interpretation

Communicate findings through maps, tables and documented outputs, stating uncertainty, assumptions and limitations.

02 · Analytical methods

Methods selected for the question

SEL uses spatial, environmental, population, infrastructure and measurement methods selected according to the research question, geography, available evidence and analytical objective.

01

Spatial analysis

Geographic techniques used to understand how conditions vary across space and how places relate to one another.

  • Spatial distribution
  • Proximity and distance
  • Accessibility
  • Spatial joins
  • Overlay analysis
  • Network analysis
  • Geographic comparison

02

Environmental & land-use analysis

Spatial approaches to environmental conditions, land systems, climate exposure and natural hazards.

  • Land-use / land-cover change
  • Forest loss
  • Environmental quality
  • Climate risk
  • Heat exposure
  • Natural hazards
  • Coastal exposure

03

Population & socioeconomic analysis

Analysis of the spatial distribution of people, living conditions, housing and socioeconomic characteristics.

  • Demographic analysis
  • Quality-of-life indicators
  • Housing conditions
  • Population vulnerability
  • Socioeconomic analysis
  • Spatial inequality

04

Energy & infrastructure analysis

Geographic approaches to infrastructure, renewable-energy potential and spatial suitability.

  • Renewable-energy potential
  • Infrastructure planning
  • Spatial suitability
  • Energy–housing relationships

05

Indicators & composite measures

Comparative measures may combine transformed variables using documented rules appropriate to the research question.

  • Indicator construction
  • Normalisation
  • Weighting
  • Scoring
  • Composite indices
  • Comparative measures
03 · Research quality

Evidence that can be understood

Analytical quality depends on whether the geography, time period, measurement, assumptions and validation approach are appropriate to the question and clearly documented.

01

Geographic consistency

Boundaries, coordinate systems and spatial units should be appropriate to the analytical question.

02

Temporal consistency

Datasets should represent compatible periods or their temporal differences should be explicitly documented.

03

Measurement

The evidence should make clear what is being measured and at what geographic scale.

04

Assumptions

Thresholds, classifications, weights and transformations should be identifiable and documented.

05

Validation

Analytical outputs should be reviewed and checked against appropriate evidence and known constraints.

06

Uncertainty & limitations

Findings should state what the available evidence cannot reasonably establish.

04 · Reproducibility

Transparent by design

Where practical, SEL documents data preparation, analytical steps, scripts, GIS projects, maps, assumptions and limitations so research can be inspected, understood and reproduced.

01

Workflow

Research stages and analytical steps are structured so the route from question to result can be followed.

02

Methods

Analytical transformations, calculations and spatial operations are documented where practical.

03

Assumptions

Important decisions, thresholds, weights, uncertainties and limitations are made explicit.

04

Outputs

Maps, indicators, reports and supporting research materials provide inspectable evidence of the analytical result.
